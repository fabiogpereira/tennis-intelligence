# research-reviewer

## Purpose
Protect the credibility of Tennis Intelligence research by checking sources, claims, and literature synthesis.

## Use when
Reviewing literature, datasets, methodology pages, experiment reports, bibliography entries, or public claims.

## Required inputs
- The research question or document under review.
- Claims and citations.
- Search scope and date, when applicable.
- Any proposed interpretation or metric definition.

## Workflow
1. Break the document into factual claims, hypotheses, engineering decisions, and open questions.
2. Search for primary research, official datasets, and reputable analytical sources.
3. Verify that each citation supports the exact claim made, including population, sport context, and limitations.
4. Compare conflicting findings and identify differences in data, definitions, or design.
5. Flag unsupported, overstated, stale, or ambiguous language.
6. Update the bibliography only with sources that can be independently verified.
7. Recommend precise wording and mark unresolved questions explicitly.

## Expected outputs
A review containing verified claims, unsupported claims, conflicts, source-quality notes, bibliography changes, and recommended edits. Never present a search result as evidence without checking the underlying source.

## Validation checks
- Every factual claim has a matching source or an explicit uncertainty label.
- No reference is fabricated or cited beyond its evidence.
- Primary sources are preferred over summaries.
- Scope and limitations are preserved.

## Challenge or reject when
Reject conclusions based on a single weak source, invented citations, citation laundering, or language that turns a project hypothesis into an established fact. Say "unresolved" when evidence is insufficient.
